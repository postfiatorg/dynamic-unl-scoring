# Scoring Prompt v9 — Structural Sub-Score Fixes

`prompts/scoring_v9.txt` replaces `scoring_v8.txt` as the active scoring prompt, shipping together with two request-assembly changes in `scoring_service/services/prompt_builder.py`: the per-validator current-UNL membership flag is no longer rendered into the model request, and the request now carries precomputed provider-family and country concentration counts produced by the new `scoring_service/services/provider_families.py` module. `scoring_v8.txt` is retained on disk for audit. Score formula v1, its weights, the selector, and churn control are unchanged; validator sidecars require no action because they replay the foundation's frozen `model_request.json` verbatim.

## The three defects, measured on testnet round 16

The first testnet round under the deterministic formula (round 16, 2026-07-28) was audited validator-by-validator against its frozen evidence. The formula layer behaved exactly (0/51 recomputation mismatches, the consensus+25 gate correctly capping a recovered-outage validator at 41), and the consensus sub-score followed a consistent rule (≈ the worst agreement window × 100). Three defects live above the formula, inside the model's sub-scores:

1. **Incumbent bias in reliability.** Among validators with consensus ≥ 99, a verified domain, and 30-day agreement ≥ 0.999, all 19 current-UNL incumbents received reliability 90 while 6 of 8 non-incumbents received 85 — including the starkest inversion: a challenger with 7 missed ledgers in 852k (the best 30-day record in the challenger set) at 85 against an incumbent with 2,427 missed at 90. Prompt v8 carried three explicit guardrails against exactly this, and the model overrode all of them. The rendered `"unl"` field is the only channel through which the model can see membership.
2. **Domain status double-counted into reliability.** v8 explicitly instructed domain verification into both reliability and identity. Under the model-owned holistic score that cross-dimension bleed was harmless; under fixed formula weights it mechanically double-counts: domain status was worth roughly 8 final points (20-point reliability swing × 20% + 35-point identity swing × 10%) instead of identity's designed 3.5. Round 16: no-domain validators with perfect agreement received reliability 70 against 85–90 for verified peers with identical operational evidence.
3. **Diversity contradicts actual concentration.** The model had to count provider representation across 51 validators in one pass. Round 16: Contabo Germany (3 set members) tied Hetzner Germany (17 members of a 23-member family) at diversity 70; the unique CABLE ONE US validator (50) tied the six-member Vultr US bloc and sat 25 points under the equally-unique HOSTKEY US validator (75); the five-member Hetzner Finland group (85) tied unique-country Vultr Australia/Japan. The identity sub-score, by contrast, mapped its four evidence states perfectly consistently — the model is reliable at per-validator judgment and unreliable at set-wide counting.

## The v9 changes — structural, not instructional

Round 16 is direct evidence that instructions do not restrain these behaviors, so v9 removes the inputs and split responsibilities instead:

1. **The model no longer sees UNL membership.** `MODEL_HIDDEN_FIELDS = {"unl"}` in the prompt builder excludes the flag from rendered validator entries; it stays in the frozen `inputs/validator_evidence.json` for audit. Selection continuity was never the model's job — the deterministic selector's churn control owns it. All per-validator membership guardrails and evidence mentions are deleted from the template (the selector-context line describing churn control's incumbent continuity remains — it describes the selector, not evidence); the model cannot be biased by evidence it never receives. Verified consumers of membership are unaffected: the selector and the sidecar's vendored selector read `inputs/previous_unl.json`, and the explorer's promoted/displaced deltas read the prior round's `unl.json` artifact.
2. **Reliability is operational evidence only.** The v9 reliability section scores sustained participation, missed-ledger tails, and outage/recovery patterns across the agreement windows, and states that accountability evidence must not move the reliability sub-score. The penalty policy for missing/unverified domains now touches identity alone. Domain status counts exactly once, at identity's published 10% weight.
3. **Concentration is counted in code, judged by the model.** `provider_families.py` groups ASNs into provider families with three layers: generic name normalization (case, punctuation, legal suffixes, numeric ASN decorations, trailing country codes — so "CONTABO, DE" and "Contabo Inc., US" unify with no Contabo-specific rule), a three-entry explicit mapping for corporate families whose names share no token (Hetzner's dedicated+cloud ASNs, Vultr/"The Constant Company", Akamai/Linode), and an own-family fallback so a never-before-seen provider automatically forms its own family with no code change. The builder renders a `NETWORK CONCENTRATION` block (families, countries, unresolved-endpoint count; deterministically ordered) plus a per-entry `provider_family` field, and the v9 diversity section instructs the model to score from those counts and never count the entries itself. The failure modes of the mapping are bounded and visible: a multi-name family without a rule counts as separate smaller families (slightly overstating its diversity), and an unrelated provider sharing a rule token would be absorbed into that family (slightly understating its diversity) — token-boundary matching keeps that direction rare. Both correct with a one-line public mapping change, and the rendered counts freeze into every round's input package for audit.

4. **Hardened consensus/reliability definitions and banded judgment sub-scores.** Replay iterations exposed that redefining reliability had left dimension ownership of a recovered outage ambiguous: one roll scored a validator with a 59% 24-hour window at consensus 100, moving the whole penalty into reliability. v9 therefore states ownership explicitly — consensus scores the validation record across all three windows and must never exceed the worst window (integer semantics; machine-checked by the harness after every run), while reliability reads where the losses sit in time by comparing the windows (only-30d = old resolved incident; 24h = recent or chronic; 1h = happening now) with identity-style guide bands per band. A companion rule requires reliability, software, diversity, and identity sub-scores to be multiples of 5 — consensus alone stays full-resolution against the numeric evidence — trading meaningless one-point distinctions for cross-roll stability, following the identity dimension's banded consistency evidence. Diversity additionally carries an isolation rule (counts only; agreement, domain, and software evidence must not move it), which eliminated quality-into-diversity bleed observed in an intermediate roll.

## Validation methodology

`scripts/replay_prompt_variants.py` renders five request variants from a completed round's frozen inputs: `baseline` (v8 exactly), three single-change variants (`no-unl`, `reliability`, `concentration` — each v8 plus exactly one v9 change), and `combined` (v9 exactly). Single-change templates are derived from `scoring_v8.txt` by exact string edits that each assert their anchor occurs exactly once (`scripts/prompt_variants/`, committed), so a variant cannot silently drift from "v8 plus one change". Two fidelity gates protect the evidence chain: the round-16 `baseline` render must equal the round's frozen `model_request.json` byte-for-byte (it does), and every variant's rendered validator entries must equal the frozen entries modulo exactly the fields that variant deliberately hides or adds — so a variant that keeps `unl` has its `unl` values checked too — including on round 15, whose frozen request predates v8 and therefore cannot match at the full-message level.

Seven replays ran against the round's pinned production runtime (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B-FP8` revision `e89b16eb…`, deterministic SGLang profile, temperature 0, the frozen request's own sampling parameters): baseline, the three singles, and combined on round 16; combined repeated on round 16 for infrastructure determinism (that repeat was later superseded by the addendum's regression-round repeat when the combined template changed); combined on round 15 for a second data shape. Raw outputs are committed under `docs/promptv9-replays/`. Pairwise variant combinations were deliberately not pre-run: an interaction effect is detectable in the combined run's per-dimension checks, and would be isolated with a targeted pair replay only if one demanded explanation. The only combined-only effects that appeared were the class-uniform software/identity band recalibrations documented under Results — order-preserving shifts that needed no pair isolation.

## Results

Raw outputs: `docs/promptv9-replays/` (one JSON per run; `content_sha256` is the determinism fingerprint). The committed combined runs are the final template after the integer-ceiling addendum below; the three singles are the original ablation evidence, and `machine-checks.txt` is the harness's check output over the committed runs. Validation was iterative and the harness gates earned their place twice: one intermediate wording produced a response with a single corrupted validator key (caught by the parse gate, reworded), and one intermediate roll surfaced the consensus-ownership ambiguity (caught by inspection, fixed by the hardened definitions, now machine-checked). All committed runs parse completely with zero errors; the committed determinism repeat is the regression round's (`r322_regression` / `r322_regression_repeat`, byte-identical).

- **The harness reproduces production bit-for-bit.** The round-16 `baseline` replay's content is exactly equal to the round's published `outputs/model_response.json` raw response, so every variant delta is attributable to the prompt alone.
- **Incumbent bias: eliminated, with single-change causal proof.** Baseline: all 19 incumbents reliability 90, challenger mean 86.2. The `no-unl` single change alone collapses both groups to exactly 85.0/85.0. Final committed combined run: 97.4/95.0 — the residual is 5-point band steps carried by real missed-ledger tails, not membership (which the model no longer sees).
- **Domain coupling: eliminated.** Baseline gap ~20 points; the `reliability` single change alone: 1.2; final committed run: 1.7 (96.7 verified vs 95.0 no-domain, band-step noise over real tails). Domain status now moves only identity, halving its former ~8-point final-score influence to the designed ~3.5.
- **Consensus: record semantics hold where they matter.** In the committed runs the gray-zone validators score exactly their records — 58 for the 59%-day validator, 65 for the 66%-month one, 16 for the severe-outage case, 83 for the old-outage case — with zero ceiling violations under the tolerance check, and overall drift against production of 0.39 mean |delta|. Reliability tells the stability story alongside (recent instability 40, old resolved incident 80).
- **Banded sub-scores: fully obeyed.** Zero multiples-of-5 violations across all committed runs. The class-uniform software band recalibration (90→100 for all 48 current-software validators, 70→80 for the three outdated) appears in this roll as in most rolls — order-preserving and harmless under the formula.
- **Diversity: identical-situation and structural fixes hold; band placement remains the noisiest surface.** Committed round-16 run: HOSTKEY US and CABLE ONE US identical at 60 (the round-15-era 25-point split stays dead), Contabo Germany 45 strictly above the Hetzner Germany bloc at 30, Hetzner Finland 50 well below unique-country Vultr Australia 85 and Japan 70, and one apex tie (Cherry Singapore and the unique Akamai Japan validator both at 90, where strict dominance would order them). Strict dominance inversions vary by roll — 54 to 131 out of 541 comparable pairs across the validated rolls — every one of them a single 5-point band; the deterministic-diversity escalation remains the guarantee-grade fix.
- **Identity:** the fourteen no-domain validators move 45→50 uniformly (inside the stated guide); verified stays 80; the single domain-present-but-verification-null validator is the one unstable class, landing at 50, 75, or 80 depending on the roll (75→80 in the committed run).
- **Selection: the committed run reproduces the published round-16 UNL exactly.** The two unique-US challengers land at finals 93 and 92 against the lowest incumbents at 90 — inside the 5-point churn gap, so continuity holds. Across validated rolls the same boundary produced a seat for one challenger, the other, or neither (see the addendum's second finding); every observed outcome is evidence-fair, and the churn gap defaults to continuity in the close case.
- **Round 15 (second data shape).** Signatures as above, zero ceiling and band violations, clean parse; consensus drift against the v7-era production baseline reflects v7→v9 rule changes, with software and identity drift zero.

## Addendum — the integer-ceiling fix (devnet round 322)

The first live v9 round, devnet 322 (2026-07-29, manual trigger, `reanchor=false`), failed at parsing: the model emitted fractional consensus sub-scores (99.993/99.994/99.997) for all three validators. Reproduced bit-for-bit offline from the round's frozen request, the cause is the ceiling rule colliding with the integer response schema on a set whose worst windows are fractional near the top: forbidden to write 100 and unwilling to drop to 99, the model wrote the fraction. The round failed closed — no Validator List work began and the standing schedule was untouched.

The fix is one clause added to the otherwise unchanged ceiling sentence: the sub-score is always a whole number, never a decimal. An intermediate wording that also added "rounded up" language with worked examples measurably weakened the ceiling (validators at 97.7 began receiving 100 — the example's generosity generalized) and was discarded; the shipped wording is the minimal one. The harness's data-fidelity gate was extended to rounds frozen under v9 itself, whose entries already carry the builder-added `provider_family` field, making round 322's frozen data a permanent regression input.

Revalidation on the final template (committed runs): round 322 parses 3/3 with integer consensus 99 for all three validators — the clause holds literally on the regression round, flooring the fractional 99.99x ceilings — with a repeat whose response content is byte-identical; testnet rounds 16 and 15 parse 51/51 and 50/50 with zero errors and round the same fractional top up to 100 instead. The model resolves the fractional-top case either way between rolls; the machine check therefore enforces the ceiling with a deliberate one-point tolerance (worst window rounded up to a whole number), which both resolutions satisfy, while degraded validators (66-ceiling, 59-ceiling, and offline-zero cases) hold the ceiling exactly in every committed run. Zero band violations anywhere. Two notes for auditors reading the artifacts: `machine-checks.txt` compares round 322 against itself (no v8 baseline exists for a round frozen under v9 — the drift lines there are structural zeros), and the three regression-round validators tie at 99 despite strictly different fractional evidence because the integer scale cannot express a distinction under their shared ceiling — a deliberate tie-at-the-ceiling resolution of the strict-monotonicity rule, to be scoped explicitly in the next revalidated prompt revision.

Two findings from the iteration rolls, documented for the roadmap. First, on validators whose windows disagree strongly, prose ceilings bind in most but not all generation rolls (one discarded roll scored a 66-ceiling validator at 94); the committed template's roll is fully clean, and the machine check now guards every future replay, but the guarantee-grade fix is computing the consensus bound in code — promoted from future work to the next scoring milestone alongside deterministic diversity. Second, the round-16 selection boundary is genuinely roll-sensitive: across four combined rolls, the seat contested by the two equally-situated unique-US challengers went to one, the other, or neither (finals hovering 92–95 against incumbents at 90 with a 5-point churn gap), so the first live v9 rounds may or may not change a seat — every observed outcome is evidence-fair, and the churn gap defaults to continuity in the close case.

## Rollout

Deployment and the first v9 rounds are tracked separately: devnet first with a manually triggered round (`reanchor=false`), then testnet on its standing weekly schedule with no manual trigger.
